# Prototype Machining in Seattle

Prototype machining is different from production machining. The goal is to make the next test possible, learn quickly, and avoid spending time on details that do not affect the current decision. Future Present Labs helps Seattle teams machine prototype parts with that mindset.

A prototype may need production-like material, real threads, bearing fits, flatness, or stiffness. It may also only need to prove clearance, ergonomics, assembly sequence, or sensor placement. The process should match the question the prototype is supposed to answer.

## Prototype machining work

- First functional hardware parts from CAD or drawings
- Rev A, Rev B, and quick iteration parts for engineering teams
- Machined fixtures for testing, assembly, and inspection
- Metal or plastic prototypes that need realistic strength, threads, or tolerances

## Practical review before cutting

We look for features that can slow a prototype down: deep pockets, tiny radii, unnecessary tight tolerances, hard-to-hold geometry, missing thread callouts, and material choices that do not match the test. When a printed part or [waterjet-cut blank](/seattle-manufacturing/waterjet-cutting-seattle/) gets you the answer faster, we will say that.

## Local feedback loop

Seattle prototype machining is useful when the designer and machinist need to talk. You can send a partial model, update after the first fit check, and keep revisions moving without waiting on a distant supplier queue.

## What makes a prototype quote useful

Tell us what must be true after the part arrives. Does it need to survive load testing? Hold an optical alignment? Fit inside a customer demo? Prove a hole pattern? Replace a production casting for one test cycle? That context changes material choice, tolerances, finish, and how aggressively we simplify the first version.
