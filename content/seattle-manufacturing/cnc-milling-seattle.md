## Aluminum CNC machining

Most local prototype work starts in aluminum because it machines cleanly, holds threads well, and gives hardware teams production-like stiffness without the cost of harder materials. We regularly work around 6061 and 7075-style use cases: brackets, plates, clamps, enclosures, fixture bases, and parts that need a clean anodize-ready surface from a finishing partner.

If the job needs stainless, titanium, brass, Delrin, or another engineering plastic, the setup changes. Tooling, feeds, holding strategy, and tolerance expectations should match the material instead of treating every prototype like an aluminum part.

## Design details that affect milling cost

Cost usually follows setup count, tool access, tolerance stackup, and inspection effort. Deep pockets, sharp internal corners, very thin walls, tiny cutters, and tight tolerances on every surface can all slow a part down. A part that looks simple in CAD may still require multiple operations if critical features are on opposite faces or if there is no good way to hold it.

The best drawings call out only the dimensions that matter. If a bearing bore, dowel hole, gasket surface, or mounting face is critical, say so. If the rest of the part only needs to be practical and clean, that helps us focus machining time where it changes the result.

## What to send

A STEP file plus a PDF drawing is ideal. Include material, quantity, finish expectations, threaded holes, inserts, critical tolerances, and the mating parts if fit matters. If you only have a sketch or an existing part, we can help scope the CAD work before cutting.
