# CNC Machining for Eastside Seattle Hardware Teams

Future Present Labs supports Eastside Seattle teams that need prototype and short-run CNC machining without handing the project to a distant, high-volume vendor. We work with engineers, founders, product teams, aerospace suppliers, robotics groups, and embedded hardware builders around Redmond, Kirkland, Bellevue, and the broader Puget Sound area.

The Eastside has a dense mix of hardware startups, aerospace engineering, robotics, medical device work, software-adjacent labs, and product teams that need physical parts to keep pace with design changes. A local CNC partner helps when the job needs direct engineering discussion, fast revision feedback, or practical manufacturability review before the next test.

## Good fit Eastside CNC work

- Prototype brackets, mounts, enclosures, panels, plates, and adapters
- Aerospace and robotics fixtures, test hardware, and short-run components
- Aluminum, stainless, brass, titanium, and engineering plastic parts
- Rev A, Rev B, and bridge parts where design feedback matters
- Small production runs that need part marking, inspection notes, or assembly support

## Why a Seattle shop can still be the right Eastside partner

For Eastside teams, the practical question is not whether the machine is inside Bellevue or Redmond city limits. It is whether the machinist can review your CAD, understand the assembly, flag tolerance or fixturing risks, and turn the next version quickly. Our Belltown shop is close enough for local pickup, direct communication, and fast iteration across the Seattle and Eastside hardware corridor.

We can also combine machining with [waterjet cutting](/seattle-manufacturing/waterjet-cutting-seattle/), [laser engraving](/seattle-manufacturing/laser-engraving-seattle/), [PCB design](/seattle-manufacturing/pcb-design-seattle/), and [fixture design](/seattle-manufacturing/fixture-design-seattle/) when a prototype needs more than a single machined part.

## What to send

Send CAD, drawings, material preferences, quantity, timing, and the reason the part matters. If the part belongs to an aerospace, robotics, test, or embedded hardware assembly, include the surrounding constraints: mating parts, critical fits, cable paths, sensor locations, load cases, or inspection requirements. That context helps us choose the fastest path to a useful part.
